# TrueWay Founder Directives: The 10 Field Realities

**Guiding Rule: Research feels like progress, but it is procrastination with good intentions. Code, field contact, and real pilgrims decide whether TrueWay lives or dies.**

- **Document Version:** 1.0 — Founder & Operational Realities
- **Effective Date:** 2026-10-11
- **Status:** Binding Operational Mandate
- **Scope:** Supersedes theoretical roadmaps; governs founder time, physical presence, budget allocation, and the transition from planning to building.

---

## The 10 Non-Negotiable Directives

### 1. Stop Planning. Start Building. This Week.
* **The Trap:** Blueprint $\rightarrow$ Deep Research $\rightarrow$ Architecture $\rightarrow$ Datasets $\rightarrow$ Multi-Agent Plan. Continuous specification creates the illusion of momentum.
* **The Rule:** **Planning is closed.** Within 7 days, you must be writing code in the repository or on the ground in Bihar collecting data. No more research reports.

---

### 2. Go to Bihar Before Building the Full App
* **The Action:** Spend 5–7 days on-site across Bodh Gaya, Rajgir, and Nalanda before scaling feature development.
* **What to Observe on the Ground:**
  * **Pilgrim Phone Demographics:** Check what hardware visitors actually carry (expect ₹8,000–₹15,000 Android devices with 32–64GB storage, directly confirming the Tier-E-first architecture).
  * **Actual Information Seeking:** Do visitors want historical narratives *before* entering, *during* the walk, or *after* at their hotel?
  * **Security & Device Rules:** Inspect the cloakroom phone deposit workflow at Mahabodhi and security checkpoints at Nalanda.
  * **GPS Behavior:** Test location jitter near ancient stone walls and dense tree cover.
* **Outcome:** You will invalidate 20% of the assumptions in the blueprint. Design from physical observation, not document synthesis.

---

### 3. The Monastery Guesthouse Distribution Hack
* **The Problem:** The "pre-trip download chicken-and-egg dilemma"—a tourist who discovers TrueWay at an internet-dead temple gate cannot download a 45 MB pack.
* **The Solution (Bodh Gaya Specific):** Bodh Gaya hosts dozens of **international monastery guesthouses** (Thai, Bhutanese, Tibetan, Japanese, Sri Lankan, Burmese, Vietnamese, Korean).
  * Pilgrims stay overnight, have high-speed Wi-Fi, and plan the next morning's temple visit.
  * Deploy laminated A4 counter cards at reception desks with a single QR code:  
    *"Download TrueWay — The Buddhist Circuit guide that works inside the temple without internet."*
  * **Reciprocal Value:** List the guesthouse, meditation hours, and visitor guidelines in the app's verified data directory.
  * **Priority:** Prioritize guesthouse partnerships *higher* than government MOUs for pilot distribution—they close in 15 minutes, require zero bureaucracy, and deliver users with Wi-Fi ready to download.

---

### 4. Trademark Clearance: Immediate Priority
* **The Risk:** Historical precedent exists for commercial use of "TrueWay" (e.g. transit data APIs), and generic names risk trademark conflict.
* **The Action:** Spend ₹10,000–₹20,000 on formal trademark and domain conflict checks with an IP attorney this week. Resolve any conflict now rather than undergoing an emergency rebrand in month 6.

---

### 5. Validate the Content Before the App
* **The Core Truth:** If travelers don't care about the *historical stories*, no amount of on-device neural vision or vector search will save the product.
* **The Test:** The initial proof-of-value can be delivered as clean, bilingual, citation-grounded PDF cards or a one-page web preview to the first 10 travelers in Bodh Gaya. If they read and value the stories, engineering the offline app is justified.

---

### 6. The Local Honorarium Content Network
* **The Problem:** Writing verified, citation-grounded claims for 50–70 POIs is the true operational bottleneck that solo founders cannot scale alone.
* **The Solution:** Partner with local scholarship in Bihar:
  * History and archaeology postgraduates at **Nalanda University**, **Magadh University (Bodh Gaya)**, and **Patna University**, plus retired ASI field archaeologists.
  * Offer an honorarium of **₹2,000 – ₹5,000 per well-researched place card draft** (including primary bibliography anchors).
  * Founder reviews and edits; local scholars provide nuance and cultural accuracy.
  * **Budget:** ₹1,00,000 – ₹1,50,000 covers the entire 50–70 POI pilot while building relationships with domain experts who vouch for TrueWay's authority.

---

### 7. Compress Kill-Tests to 4 Weeks
* **The Revision:** Six weeks for Phase 0 feasibility testing is 25% of the total budget—too long.
* **Compressed Target:**
  * **KT-001 (5-Monument Vision Feasibility):** 3 weeks.
  * **KT-002 (First Complete Mahabodhi Card):** 1 week.
  * **KT-003 (First Guesthouse/Partner LOI):** 4 weeks.
* If 5-monument recognition cannot pass in 3–4 weeks, demote camera recognition to P1 immediately and advance with FTS search and signed QR touchpoints as P0.

---

### 8. The Single North Star Metric
Forget vanity metrics (downloads, impressions, abstract top-1 scores). Track one number on a sticky note:

$$\mathbf{\text{North Star Metric}} = \frac{\text{Sessions Where User Reads a Full Place Card}}{\text{Total App Sessions}}$$

If travelers read the complete historical story and explore the cited claims, every other problem is solvable. If they bounce after 10 seconds, the product is failing.

---

### 9. Protect Your Code Review Capacity
* **The Bottleneck:** AI agents write code far faster than you can review it. Founder review throughput determines the delivery velocity of the entire project.
* **Practical Protocol:**
  * Batch pull request reviews **twice a day** (e.g., 11:00 AM and 5:00 PM), not continuously.
  * Mute agent notifications during deep architectural work.
  * **Never merge code you do not fully understand.** Require the agent to explain or simplify any obscure logic.
  * Avoid watching terminal agent output in real-time; rely on automated CI and background task completions.

---

### 10. Immutable Definition of Pilot Success
Do not move the goalposts in week 23 under stress. The definition of pilot success is locked today in writing:

> **"The pilot succeeds if 30 real users complete one full journey — find a place (via camera, search, or QR), read its story, find a second place — and at least 10 of them explicitly say they would recommend TrueWay to another traveler."**
